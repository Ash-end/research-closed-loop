# Manage research directions and uncertainty

Buy useful options at affordable cost rather than run every direction at full speed. Two or three independent ideas are optional; one main line and a written backup can be enough.

## Procedure

1. Read actual human, compute, time, data, and deadline constraints. Do not invent budgets. List the main line and existing alternatives rather than force two additional ideas.
2. Compare problem value, mechanism evidence, next discriminating test, cost, dependencies, and failure modes. Shared data or unverified mechanisms correlate risk; do not label such directions independent without qualification.
3. Assign main, low-cost backup, deferred, or stopped status. Prioritize information gained per practical cost. Parallelism depends on resources and dependencies, not a quota or fabricated success probabilities.
4. Define switches: refuted central hypothesis, benefit below cost, persistently unavailable data/permissions, or exhausted budget/deadline. A transient software error does not automatically justify abandoning a line. Revise prior decisions when evidence changes, with reasons; sunk cost is not a justification to persist.
5. Check shared risks and resource conflict. Avoid contaminating the same test set or contending for devices. For requested reviews, report changes and next decisions; do not impose daily logging, automatic wakeups, endless retries, or unrequested execution.

## Deliverable

Adapt the [portfolio board](assets/portfolio-board.md). Explain the main line, backup maintenance cost, shared risks, next test, and switch/stop conditions. A single direction may remain the focus. Distinguish recommended from actually executed parallel work.

This is research decision support, not permission for additional agents, training, remote jobs, paid calls, or scheduled background work. Actual execution needs current authorization and budget. No competition award standards or success guarantees.
